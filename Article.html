Club History & Vision

Our Beginnings

Article

Kshitij began with a simple idea at IISER Pune: why not build a rocket ourselves? As a group of students interested in space, engineering and hands on projects, we wanted to move beyond just studying concepts and actually experience what it takes to design, build and test a rocket. That idea eventually led to the formation of Team Kshitij, the first student led rocketry team among the IISERs.

Start of the journey

Our journey began with the IN SPACe Model Rocketry India Student Competition 2026 to 27. The competition gave us a reason to bring together students from different areas and work towards one common goal. What started as a rocket design soon became a complete aerospace project involving aerodynamics, propulsion, structural design, avionics, telemetry, flight software, recovery systems and a CanSat payload. We had to learn how all these different systems work individually and, more importantly, how they work together.

Learning through Design and Testing

A lot of our time went into designing, simulating, discussing, testing and redesigning. Things that looked simple on paper often turned out to be much more complicated when we started thinking about actually fabricating and testing them. That process taught us an important part of engineering. The first design is rarely the final design. Every problem gave us something new to understand and improve. One of the major stages of our journey was the PDR and CDR process. We had to present our rocket and explain the decisions behind our design to experienced scientists and technical experts from the ISRO and IN SPACe ecosystem. The reviews were a great learning experience because the questions went beyond whether something would work. We had to think about why it would work, how we would test it, what could go wrong and how we would make the system more reliable.

We also got opportunities to interact with people from the Indian space sector outside the regular project work. On the occasion of National Space Day, our team was invited to Delhi, where we got the opportunity to meet people from ISRO and discuss and present our rocket designs and project work. Experiences like these made the journey even more meaningful and gave us a better understanding of the kind of engineering that goes into real space missions. Throughout this journey, our focus has remained on turning our designs into a real, working system. From the rocket structure and propulsion to avionics, telemetry, recovery and the CanSat, every subsystem has required careful planning and continuous improvement. We are now continuing this work with a strong focus on procurement, fabrication, integration and extensive ground testing at IISER Pune. This journey has also been possible because of the support and guidance we have received from people and organisations who believe in what we are trying to build. Mr. Sankar Venkatraman is supporting us as our benefactor with a contribution of ₹1,00,000. Thrust Tech India is supporting us as a manufacturing partner for rocket avionics and related components, while Renishaw India, Pune is supporting us with metal 3D printing for selected components. We are also grateful for the continuous guidance and encouragement of our faculty mentors, Prof. Prasad Subramanian and Prof. Bhas Bapat, who have supported us throughout our rocketry journey.

What Kshitij is at its core

"At its core, Team Kshitij is about learning by actually doing. It is about taking an idea, trying to turn it into something real, finding out what does not work, fixing it and trying again. Most of us had never built a rocket before starting this project, and there is still a lot for us to learn. But that is exactly what makes the journey exciting."

We started with an idea. We built a team, designed our rocket, went through technical reviews, learned from every challenge and continued developing our systems. Now, we are working towards our next major milestone: completing our testing and launching our first rocket. The name Kshitij means horizon. For us, it represents the idea of always looking beyond what has already been achieved and trying to go a little further. Our journey has only just begun. Our first launch is coming soon.
